# Index allocation research notes — 2026-11-15

## v1.1 Midcap150 Momentum 50, volatility target 20% + own 200-day average, weekly

**Training outcome:** 1.46%/month (17.5%/yr; NIFTY 50 12.2%), worst fall 29.5%, luck t 2.69, invested 70% of the time - fails: averaged 1.46% a month (the target is 1.5%)

**Lessons:** Midcap150 momentum-50 with a volatility target controls the fall well, with lots of headroom (29.5% vs 59.5% for the benchmark). The binding constraint is return, not risk. Return is lost to cash drag (70% invested, beta 0.44) and to whipsaws in mild bull years (2016, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2025). The volatility target can afford to be looser. Do not tighten risk controls further on this idea. If loosening the target pushes the fall past the limit, the 2008 and 2011 rows will show it. Second-half alpha decay (5.66%) is worth watching.

Changes that raise exposure or cut whipsaw are what move return and t. Alpha is decaying in the second half, so watch it.

## v1.3 Midcap150 Momentum 50, volatility target 30% + own 200-day average, weekly

**Training outcome:** 1.50%/month (17.9%/yr; NIFTY 50 12.2%), worst fall 33.3%, luck t 2.56, invested 74% of the time - fails: its edge could be luck: t = 2.56 (needs 2.6)

**Lessons:** Midcap150 momentum with vol targeting reliably fixes the fall check; drawdown is not the problem. The binding constraint is t-stat, driven by momentum-reversal years (2016, 2018-19, 2022, 2025) where stock-level picks lose while the index rises. The fix is lower residual variance or turnover, not a tighter fall control. Do not nudge lookback or vol-target numbers just to clear 2.6 by 0.04, because a pass found that way is likely overfit. Alpha is decaying over time (10.3 to 5.8), so be skeptical of any version whose edge sits mainly before 2015.
