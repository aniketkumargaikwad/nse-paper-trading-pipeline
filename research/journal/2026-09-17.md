# Research notes — 2026-09-17

## 60m day-high continuation, gated by 200-day trend + rising slope

**Training outcome:** 283 of 1177 combinations profitable after fees, net -41329503

**Lessons:** A rule whose gross PnL is ~0 per trade cannot be rescued by tuning exits, sizing or slots - four versions have confirmed the entry carries no information. Trade count is the dominant cost driver; any future idea must clear fees by a wide margin per trade before anything else matters, which rules out sub-hourly bars for this fee model. Extreme drawdowns at short timeframes point to a position/loss-control defect as well as a signal defect. Against a basket holding at ~1.9%/month, the 5-7% monthly target needs a rule that is invested most of the time with a genuine per-trade edge, not one taking hundreds of low-conviction trades a month. Testing 1,177 combos and celebrating 60 that beat hold is selection noise.

## Leader breakout to a 200-day high, held on a wide give-back trail

**Training outcome:** 877 of 1177 combinations profitable after fees, net 100855312

**Lessons:** The binding constraint is deployment, not signal quality. 80-83% of signals are skipped with 10 slots ~88% full, so the account holds few, long, arbitrarily chosen positions; results depend on which signal grabbed a slot, not on the rule. Positive excess while invested plus negative excess over the month is the signature of a rule that is right but too idle to matter. A rule that fires 2-3 times a month can never be told from luck over 100 months, and the bottom table shows the real cost: on compounders (BAJFINANCE, ASTRAL, KEI) selling out of an uptrend forfeits thousands of percent. Any next attempt needs more, smaller, shorter positions, or an explicit hold-through rule for names already trending.
