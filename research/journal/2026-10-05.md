# Research notes — 2026-10-05

## Leader Supertrend flip in a rising 200-SMA, ATR-capped 2-8 week swing

**Training outcome:** 93 of 198 combinations profitable after fees, net 1331849

**Lessons:** Short holds on a long-only signal in a strongly trending market (holding averaged about +1.75% a month) give up most of the drift and pay fees and whipsaw costs in sideways years. A rule that cannot beat holding while it is invested will not get better by raising slot use. The target of 5-7% a month needs a rule that stays in winners for a long time and ranks entries by strength when slots are full, rather than taking signals first-come.

## Accelerating 100-day leader breakout, 12-bar fail-fast cull, winners ride 50-EMA

**Training outcome:** 106 of 194 combinations profitable after fees, net 2818159

**Lessons:** A fixed ~20-day swing exit leaves the trend premium on the table. Holding-relative edge needs longer holds on leaders and less idle capital. The top table is just decliners being avoided, not alpha. With 5-7%/month as the target and +0.9% achieved, tuning the exit alone is unlikely to close the gap.

## Leader pullback-to-EMA20 reclaim with volume, stays in winners via EMA20 trail

**Training outcome:** 111 of 194 combinations profitable after fees, net 3887950

**Lessons:** Short ~3-4 week holds on daily bars give up the trend returns that buy-and-hold captures in this universe, and they still carry deep drawdowns. Beating hold only on collapsing stocks is avoiding losses, not an edge. A next idea should stay invested longer in persistent uptrends, rank stocks by relative strength so the 10 slots are filled with the best names instead of whichever signal arrives first, and add a market-regime filter to cut the 35% dip.

## Rising-200-SMA 100-day breakout, own-stock regime gate in place of NIFTY filter

**Training outcome:** 96 of 198 combinations profitable after fees, net 868476

**Lessons:** Short-hold swing entries on daily bars cannot keep pace with Indian mid/large-cap compounders. Holding returns average 1.75% a month, and the rule captures a fraction while paying heavy fees. Future ideas should stay invested longer: trend-following with long holds, or a market/regime filter that holds through uptrends and exits in bear phases. They should also cut turnover to reduce fees, and add a portfolio drawdown control to keep worst_dip under 30%. Beating hold on declining stocks is not evidence of edge; judge on avg_excess_active_pct and account qualification.
