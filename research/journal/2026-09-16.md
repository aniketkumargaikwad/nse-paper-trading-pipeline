# Research notes — 2026-09-16

## Gap-hold continuation: buy the unfilled gap above yesterday's high, ATR-swing exit

**Training outcome:** 677 of 1164 combinations profitable after fees, net 20433849

**Lessons:** Absolute profit is meaningless here - the entire 2020/2021/2023 return is beta captured at roughly a quarter of hold's rate while paying full fees. The negative excess appears in the baskets too, not just the accounts, so it is the entry rule itself and cannot be fixed by slot sizing, filtering or regime overlays on top of the same signal. A 37% win rate with fees at 49% of gross means the per-trade edge is smaller than the round-trip cost; anything that trades this often needs a much larger average win. The top table is survivorship of collapsed stocks, not evidence. Next idea must show positive avg_excess_pct on the basket view before any account tuning is worth doing, and must trade far less or hold far longer to clear costs.

## Daily 20-day breakout in a 200-EMA regime, ATR-trailed 2-10 day swing exits

**Training outcome:** 414 of 1164 combinations profitable after fees, net 707073

**Lessons:** Any rule that trades this often on Indian equities has to clear a fee bar that is ~90% of its gross here; trade count must come down by an order of magnitude or per-trade edge must be much larger. Beating hold on wrecks while missing compounders is a short-side/avoidance signature, not a long edge - measure against hold from the start. Drawdown is the binding constraint, not return: a 10-slot account concentrating into correlated signals gave 54-81% dips, so position sizing and a portfolio-level risk cut matter more than entry tuning. Uniform ~5.7% beat-hold rate across all six timeframes is the fingerprint of no signal; check that before tuning parameters.

## GAP-CONTINUATION-SWING: break of a high-volume 2% gap-up day, daily-ATR exits

**Training outcome:** 351 of 1164 combinations profitable after fees, net 488401

**Lessons:** Measured against a basket whose holding return averages +1.9% a month, a long-only timing rule that is flat 85% of the time cannot compete: idle capital is the dominant cost, far larger than any per-trade edge. Chasing this benchmark needs either near-full invested time or genuinely higher per-trade expectancy, not more selective filters. A 35% win rate with fees at half of gross is a signal-quality problem, not a tuning problem - no threshold sweep fixes it. Zero trades on daily bars over 187 months means the entry condition is defined on intraday microstructure and does not generalise; any future idea should be checked for firing at all before being scored. Finally, excess_vs_hold ranking flatters strategies that merely avoid delisting-grade losers - read combos_beating_hold and edge_t instead of the top table.
