# Options research notes — 2026-11-09

## Weekly NIFTY iron condor sold only when implied move beats recent realized range

**Training outcome:** NIFTY +0.01%/month, 0.1 trades/month, worst day -12.3%, luck t -2.38 - fails: worst day -12.3% (limit -10%); BANKNIFTY -0.06%/month, 0.0 trades/month, worst day -13.0%, luck t -2.06 - fails: worst day -13.0% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** An entry filter this strict produces almost no trades, so no monthly return target can be reached and no statistical edge can be shown. Per-trade loss must be capped well below 10% of capital through smaller size or defined-risk spreads. Calm-VIX or sideways entries were the losers, but with this few trades that is anecdote, not evidence. A positional idea that trades a few times a year cannot be tuned into 1.5-2% a month with one change.

## Uptrend-only weekly bull put spread, delta-scaled, fast 40% take-profit

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -0.08%/month, 0.4 trades/month, worst day -3.3%, luck t -2.76 - fails: lost money on average: -0.08% a month; BANKNIFTY -0.03%/month, 0.4 trades/month, worst day -3.5%, luck t -2.14 - fails: lost money on average: -0.03% a month

**Lessons:** A high win rate hides a negative payoff: losers in normal VIX, falling or sideways trends, and 4-15 DTE entries outweigh the winners. Entry rules this strict produce under 5 trades a year, which can never reach 1.5-2% a month, even if the per-trade edge were positive. Filters that look good on one index flip on the other, which is a sign of noise. A next idea needs to trade at least weekly, with an edge that is consistent across both indexes and a positive edge_t before any filters are added.

## Fear-day bull put spread: sell rich puts after down closes with VIX up

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -0.13%/month, 0.7 trades/month, worst day -12.6%, luck t -2.96 - fails: worst day -12.6% (limit -10%); BANKNIFTY -0.10%/month, 0.6 trades/month, worst day -15.8%, luck t -2.48 - fails: worst day -15.8% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** A short-premium, sell-and-wait idea entered this rarely cannot reach 1.5-2% a month, and its tail days break the -10% limit. A high win rate with negative edge_t means the risk-reward is structurally wrong, not mistuned. The next idea needs defined risk (spreads or wings) to cap the worst day, and many more entries (several per month) so the average month can compound. It should only be kept if edge_t is positive on both indexes. Filters fitted to small splits (trend, DTE) should not be trusted unless they hold on both NIFTY and BANKNIFTY.

## Trend-gated bullish seagull: skew-funded calls with a defined put wing

**Training outcome:** NIFTY +0.40%/month, 0.6 trades/month, worst day -11.8%, luck t -0.84 - fails: worst day -11.8% (limit -10%); BANKNIFTY +0.81%/month, 0.5 trades/month, worst day -18.0%, luck t -0.49 - fails: worst day -18.0% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** A low win rate (30-37%) with rare, large single-day losses means sizing and tail exposure dominate the result. Trading under once a month cannot reach a 1.5-2% average month. Positive split buckets this small are noise, and filtering further would cut trade count even more. The recent-year collapse on both indexes points to structural decay, not bad parameters. Daily loss must be capped by construction, for example with defined-risk structures, rather than filtered after the fact.
