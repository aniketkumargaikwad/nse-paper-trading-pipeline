# Options research notes — 2026-11-18

## Low-IV trend call debit spread, debit capped at 6% of account

**Training outcome:** NIFTY +0.17%/month, 0.7 trades/month, worst day -7.7%, luck t -2.16 - fails: too slow: 0.7 trades a month (needs 10, or 1.5% a month); BANKNIFTY -0.07%/month, 0.5 trades/month, worst day -10.1%, luck t -2.22 - fails: worst day -10.1% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** The entry filter is far too narrow, and it keeps picking low-IV setups that offer little premium. Loosening the filter would only produce more trades with an edge that is already negative, so tuning this idea is not worthwhile. The next idea needs a structure that trades roughly weekly by design and caps the loss on each trade with defined risk, such as spreads, so it stays under the -10% worst-day limit. It must also hold up on both indexes and not just in one cherry-picked condition. Wins should be judged by edge_t of 2 or more, not by win rate.

## Uptrend monthly call debit spread, delta 0.65/0.35, fast 30% target/stop

**Training outcome:** NIFTY +0.03%/month, 0.1 trades/month, worst day -4.4%, luck t -2.50 - fails: too slow: 0.1 trades a month (needs 10, or 1.5% a month); BANKNIFTY -0.04%/month, 0.0 trades/month, worst day -4.9%, luck t -2.19 - fails: lost money on average: -0.04% a month

**Lessons:** The entry conditions are far too restrictive: they effectively stop firing after 2018, which suggests a filter tied to a regime (low IV rank or VIX level) that rarely recurs. A filter that removes 99% of days cannot reach 10 trades a month, so tightening it further is pointless. The weak hints are to avoid falling-trend entries and 8-15 DTE entries, but they rest on only 16 trades. Positional holding of about 7 days also caps how often the strategy can trade. The idea needs a structurally frequent entry rather than a rare-condition trigger.

## Trend-hold bull call debit spread, 8% debit budget, fast 40/40 turnover

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -0.13%/month, 0.5 trades/month, worst day -5.5%, luck t -2.76 - fails: lost money on average: -0.13% a month; BANKNIFTY +0.13%/month, 0.4 trades/month, worst day -8.5%, luck t -1.90 - fails: too slow: 0.4 trades a month (needs 10, or 1.5% a month)

**Lessons:** A filter-heavy positional entry that fires about 5 times a year cannot reach 10 trades a month, so it cannot pass the rule whatever its per-trade quality. Narrowing to the profitable pockets (high VIX, sideways) would cut trades even further. The next idea needs a base entry that fires on most sessions, or a daily or intraday structure. Regime (VIX level, trend) should then size or skip trades rather than gate entry. Avoid entering in normal-VIX, rising-trend conditions.

## Trend-gated leveraged synthetic long with a 3% put floor, monthly

**Training outcome:** NIFTY +0.23%/month, 0.1 trades/month, worst day -18.0%, luck t -1.54 - fails: worst day -18.0% (limit -10%); BANKNIFTY -0.26%/month, 0.0 trades/month, worst day -12.1%, luck t -2.48 - fails: worst day -12.1% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** Stacking filters gives too few trades to earn returns or to test them. A strategy that trades a few times a year fails no matter how good its trades are. Risk has to be capped in how the trade is built, using defined-risk spreads and sizing so one bad day stays well under 10%. Splits drawn from 4-14 trades should not be trusted.

## Intraday NIFTY/BANKNIFTY bear call credit spread: daytime-drift plus call theta

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -8.85%/month, 15.1 trades/month, worst day -2.3%, luck t -3.01 - fails: fell 78.9% from a high (limit 35%); BANKNIFTY -15.80%/month, 10.3 trades/month, worst day -3.0%, luck t -4.47 - fails: fell 93.7% from a high (limit 35%)

**Lessons:** A trade that loses most of the time, with charges on top, compounds into a near-total wipeout even when no single day is large. Check capital and strike availability before judging any results. A skip share above 100% means skips are counted more than once per session, and the skip accounting needs fixing. Positive slices with tiny sample sizes are not an edge. Low IV rank dominated the entries, and the strategy lost money there on both indexes.
