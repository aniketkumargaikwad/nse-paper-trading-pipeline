# Options research notes — 2026-10-19

## Range-confirmed near-expiry iron fly on NIFTY (intraday)

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -3.99%/month, 7.3 trades/month, worst day -13.7%, luck t -1.42 - fails: worst day -13.7% (limit -10%); BANKNIFTY -2.41%/month, 3.0 trades/month, worst day -20.3%, luck t -1.03 - fails: worst day -20.3% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** Selling 0-3 DTE premium when IV rank is low and the market is sideways gives no edge: the premium is too cheap to pay for the intraday tail moves. Undefined or loosely defined risk near expiry produces single days of -10% to -20%, which fails the pass rule on its own. Size positions to the actual capital and pick strikes that exist in the chain, or the backtest only describes a few days. A filter on mid IV rank or trend would cut trading to a few times a year, which fails the frequency aim. The next idea needs a hard daily loss cap built in and must enter on most sessions.

## Intraday bull put spread on morning strength with falling VIX

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -1.14%/month, 6.1 trades/month, worst day -9.1%, luck t -0.70 - fails: lost money on average: -1.14% a month; BANKNIFTY -3.66%/month, 5.3 trades/month, worst day -9.1%, luck t -2.45 - fails: fell 42.2% from a high (limit 30%)

**Lessons:** The core entry loses most in its most common condition: sideways, low IV rank, normal VIX. It is not a filtering problem that one rule change can fix. Single-day losses of 7-9% show the risk per trade is too large for a 30% drawdown limit. Strike selection must be robust on BANKNIFTY (expiry and strike-grid changes) or most sessions are skipped. Positive slices with 3-22 trades should not be chased; that is overfitting.

## Intraday bull put spread on opening-range breakouts near expiry

**Training outcome:** NIFTY -4.46%/month, 4.2 trades/month, worst day -7.4%, luck t -2.46 - fails: fell 49.5% from a high (limit 30%); BANKNIFTY -2.36%/month, 1.1 trades/month, worst day -11.8%, luck t -1.26 - fails: worst day -11.8% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** Taking near-expiry trades whenever IV is low and the market is sideways has a reliably negative edge on both indexes. The edge appears only when premium is rich, and that happens too rarely to reach the trade count needed for 5-7% a month. Filtering down to those few trades would make the version fail on trade frequency. With a significantly negative edge_t after three versions, more tuning is unlikely to help. The next idea should have its edge on ordinary days, should cap daily loss by design (for example defined-risk structures), and should avoid strikes that BANKNIFTY often lacks.

## Near-expiry inside-range iron condor (NIFTY weekly)

**Training outcome:** NIFTY +1.08%/month, 10.4 trades/month, worst day -13.9%, luck t +0.39 - fails: worst day -13.9% (limit -10%); BANKNIFTY +6.04%/month, 2.4 trades/month, worst day -11.2%, luck t +2.04 - fails: worst day -11.2% (limit -10%)

**Lessons:** A high win rate does not survive one uncapped tail day. Each index's failure traces to one or two sessions, so a hard intraday loss cap is the binding constraint. A 0-3 day expiry filter starves BANKNIFTY of trades now that its weekly expiries are gone, and strike lookup fails on most days. That frequency problem is structural and one tweak will not fix it. NIFTY's edge is only noise, and the conditional splits are too thin to filter on without overfitting.
