# Research notes — 2026-09-18

## Daily-only 200-day leader breakout with an ATR cost hurdle

**Training outcome:** 594 of 1177 combinations profitable after fees, net 31017686

**Lessons:** Four versions in, the rule has never produced positive active excess on any timeframe, and each version has tuned entries without addressing the two structural faults: it is out of the market 75-93% of the time (slot_use 6.9-90%, signals_skipped 83% on day), so it can never reach 5-7% monthly on whole capital, and it exits winners early while riding losers (28% win rate with drawdowns to 120%). Fast timeframes are fee-destroyed. Chasing the one positive number (day basket) would again collide with the ten-slot funnel, where 83% of signals are dropped and the realised path is lumpy. Any successor must be designed around staying invested and a hard per-position stop, not around better signal timing.

## Leader 20-day breakout: 2.5-ATR partial target, runner on a 4-ATR trail

**Training outcome:** 402 of 1164 combinations profitable after fees, net 844593

**Lessons:** 1) Fee load, not signal quality, is the binding constraint at this trade count - 76% of gross P&L was consumed. Any future idea must either cut trades by an order of magnitude or show a per-trade edge several times the round-trip cost before backtesting broadly. 2) Judge on avg_excess_active_pct first; positive net P&L with negative active excess means the capital would do better parked in the same stocks. 3) Low slot_use with a 10-slot account guarantees the idle-capital gap can never be closed - a qualifying rule has to stay invested, not just be right occasionally. 4) A rule that systematically exits compounders is structurally short the market's main return source. 5) years_positive_pct near zero with a positive average means the average is carried by one or two years and should not be believed.
