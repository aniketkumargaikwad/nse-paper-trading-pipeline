# Research notes — 2026-10-10

## Leader 100-day breakout, own-stock golden-cross regime proxy, 50-SMA hold

**Training outcome:** 142 of 198 combinations profitable after fees, net 7261639

**Lessons:** Avoiding losses in declining stocks is not enough. In a market where holding gained about 1.75% a month, a rule that exits and re-enters loses the compounding of the leaders. With about 69% of signals skipped, ranking signals matters, but the underlying signal shows no edge (negative active excess, negative t). The next idea needs to hold strong trending or momentum stocks longer, choose the best candidates when slots are full, and keep capital fully at work so it can close the gap to holding.

## Calm 6-month leader EMA20 re-entry, held to an EMA100-minus-ATR break

**Training outcome:** 123 of 198 combinations profitable after fees, net 5186958

**Lessons:** After three versions, this entry/exit logic acts as a defensive filter, not a source of return. It cannot keep up with holding in bull years (2010, 2012, 2017, 2020, 2023) because it cuts winners and sits out trends. Getting to 5-7% a month needs high slot use, ranking among competing signals, and letting winners run in strong compounders. Avoiding decliners is not enough. The next idea should hold leaders for a long time (relative-strength rotation with slow exits) rather than time short entries.

## Strong-trend 20-day high entry, held on a 50-SMA close break / 4-ATR chandelier

**Training outcome:** 152 of 198 combinations profitable after fees, net 15538489

**Lessons:** Swing holds of 30-55 days on daily bars cannot reach the 10 trades a month the account needs. Faster exits or a shorter timeframe are required. Because 76% of signals were skipped, signals need a ranking such as strength or relative momentum rather than first-come entry. The idea adds value on weak or cyclical stocks but gives up huge returns on secular compounders. A trend filter that keeps strong-trend stocks held longer would close the gap with holding. Drawdowns near 29% and -16% months need a regime or index filter, for example staying flat when the index is below its 200-day average.
