# The options atlas — what the classic strategies earned, 2016 to Sep 2025

Measured 6 Oct 2026 by `scripts/fno_atlas.py` (step 2b of
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-fno-options-design.md`). Full reports, with
every split, in `data/fno/atlas/results.json`.

## In plain words

- **None of the eight textbook option strategies made money over the decade,
  on either index, once real costs are counted.** The best — a NIFTY short
  strangle at 20 delta — roughly broke even (+0.08% a month) and lost to just
  holding the index in every year.
- **Selling options wins often and loses big.** Short strangles won 70–78% of
  their trades, but the losing weeks were large enough to cancel the winners.
  That is the shape everyone warns about, and these numbers confirm it on
  this data.
- **One clear pattern for the AI to build on:** the straddles, strangles and
  iron condors did best when options were *expensive* — IV rank in its top
  third. Those entries won 75–100% of the time with clearly positive returns.
  They are rare (2–4% of entries), so this is a lead to test, not a result.
  The two-leg spreads did not show it.
- **Buying a straddle before Budget / RBI / election-result days lost money**
  — 74–81% of the time. Options are priced up ahead of those days.
- **A ₹5 lakh account is small for these.** After a bad run the balance can no
  longer fund even one lot, and the strategy stops trading for good. That
  happened to the plain straddles and is part of why they look so poor — and
  it is what would happen to the real account.

Against the pass rule (design §6): none qualifies. Every one fails "average
month > 0" or "beat holding the index in half its years", and the luck test.
Only the NIFTY straddle, the three NIFTY strangles and the BANKNIFTY 20- and
30-delta strangles kept their worst day inside the 10% limit; the spreads lost a quarter of the
account on single days — sized by their maximum loss, a full loss in one day
*is* a quarter of the balance.

## Strategy by strategy

- **Short straddle (ATM).** Won 54% (NIFTY) and 45% (BANKNIFTY) of trades and
  lost on average. Worst when the index was falling (NIFTY: −2.2% of the
  balance per trade), best in a rising market. NIFTY's fell 46% and
  BANKNIFTY's 61%; once the balance was that low, one lot needed more margin
  than a quarter of it, so both sat out for long stretches (NIFTY 949 skipped entries,
  BANKNIFTY 2,010) — the zeros on the named shock days are days it was not
  trading.
- **Short strangles (15/20/30 delta).** The most robust of the set: 70–78% of
  trades won at 15–20 delta, and the worst days stayed at 4.6–5.3% on NIFTY
  (8.6–12% on BANKNIFTY). Close to break-even; the 30-delta version gave
  more back. Entries when IV rank was in its top third won 82–100% of the
  time.
- **Iron condor (20-delta short, 5-delta wings).** Wins 68–69% of trades but
  loses heavily: the 5-delta wings cost ₹2–5, so one tick of spread on them is
  1–2.5% each way, and four legs pay four charges. Sized by maximum loss, a
  single bad week costs a quarter of the account (24 Feb 2022: −4.8% NIFTY,
  −26.5% BANKNIFTY; 12 Mar 2020 BANKNIFTY −21.2%).
- **Bull put spread (30/10 delta).** Most active (6.5–6.7 trades a month), 73–74%
  won; its losses cluster on crash days (24 Feb 2022: −17.8% NIFTY, −17.9%
  BANKNIFTY). Years swing from +50% to −87%: not a steady earner.
- **Bear call spread (30/10 delta).** Lost in the rising years (2017, 2021–23
  on NIFTY) as the market climbed through the short call.
- **Long straddle before events.** Lost on 74% (NIFTY) and 81% (BANKNIFTY) of
  events: premiums before scheduled events already price the move.

## What the AI should take from this

1. Option *selling* is only worth testing with a filter on how expensive
   options are (IV rank, ATM IV against VIX, expected move) and on trend —
   the plain versions do not pay for their tails.
2. Sizing decides survival. The 10% worst-day rule rules out the spreads at a
   quarter of the balance; a strategy that wants to pass must size well
   below that or cap its loss per day.
3. Buying volatility ahead of scheduled events is not an edge here.

## Read with care

- **Daily data only.** Decisions at the close, fills at the next open; stops
  are seen on closing prices only. 12 Mar 2020 is the clearest case: a NIFTY
  straddle sat 361 points down against a 432-point stop at the 9 and 11 Mar
  closes, then expired 1,228 points down after an intraday crash a minute-
  data test would have stopped out of. Minute data (step 3) tightens this.
- **Margin** is a SPAN-style estimate from NSE's rules (scan floor 6% or 0.5 ×
  VIX, 2% exposure, 3% beyond 10% out of the money, +2% on expiry day from
  20 Nov 2024), not yet checked against a broker's calculator. One NIFTY
  straddle lot came to ₹80,000 median.
- **IV rank** is skewed low: the 2020 spike sets the range for a year, so most
  entries fall in the "low third". The "top third" findings rest on 9–28
  trades per strategy.
- **Lot sizes** before July 2024 come from `fno/lot_sizes.yaml`; in the few
  weeks after a lot change the table's lot can differ from a weekly's.
- **Yearly figures** add the months up rather than compounding them, as on
  every stock result.

## Bugs this run found and fixed before these numbers

The first full run produced impossible results — a defined-risk spread losing
864% of the account in a day. Fixed before measuring:

- **Opening prints of a multi-leg position were not simultaneous.** On 23 Sep
  2019 a 150-point call spread was closed at its legs' first trades, 676 and
  450 — 226 points, more than it could ever be worth. Fills and daily marks
  now hold the legs to the position's no-arbitrage limits, moving the
  least-traded leg.
- **Holiday-moved expiries** (ten since 2016): NSE relabels the contracts, and
  a held position lost track of them. It now follows the new date.
- **Yahoo's index closes** arrive in single precision (17,610.40 as
  17,610.400390625) and fed weekly pay-outs; now rounded to the paisa.
- **During a lot change** expiries carry different lots (Dec 2024: weeklies 75,
  the old monthly 25); the engine now reads the traded expiry's own lot.
- **At 100% of the balance in margin** every short-option strategy ruined
  itself within weeks — the BANKNIFTY straddle ran at about 12× leverage and
  lost 64% in early 2016. The atlas therefore measures at a quarter.
